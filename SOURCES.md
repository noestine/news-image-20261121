## Russian missiles land in Poland (lead)
- **Source:** AP (via The Reflector / CBS News), Friday 20 November 2026;
  NATO response and Rutte's Warsaw visit, Saturday 21 November.
- Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone came down in
  south-eastern Poland during a major Russian attack on Ukraine. A Kh-101
  cruise missile fell near Nowy Lipowiec, about 50 km (30 miles) from the
  Ukraine border; a second missile near Rudnik nad Sanem; the drone near
  Mołodycz. Nobody was injured in Poland.
- Russia fired 400+ drones and 110 missiles at Ukraine overnight; at
  least 8 people killed in Ukraine (President Zelenskyy).
- PM Donald Tusk: "It does not look like an accident... we are dealing
  with an intentional provocation." NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte:
  "grave violations of Polish airspace"; allied jets scrambled. Poland
  seeks more forces for NATO's Eastern Sentry operation; Rutte due in
  Warsaw on Saturday 21 November.

## SEC probes Nvidia
- **Source:** Reuters, Friday 20 November 2026.
- The US Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia's
  accounting practices, two people briefed on the matter said. President
  Trump publicly urged Nvidia to bring back former CEO Jensen Huang;
  the board said it has no plans to reinstate him.

## Denmark foils assassination plot
- **Source:** Reuters (Copenhagen), Friday 20 November 2026.
- Danish police averted an attempt by Russian intelligence services to
  assassinate US-Ukrainian rapper Kyivstoner ahead of a concert north of
  Copenhagen on 25 September; three men with underworld links were
  arrested with two loaded pistols and two knives. Denmark expelled
  Russia's defence attache in protest.

## Riyadh intercepts 3 Houthi missiles

  21 November 2026.
- The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed three Houthi ballistic
  missiles fired at Riyadh and struck Hodeidah, a day after a Houthi
  missile hit the PIF Tower in Riyadh.

## YouTube nears $3bn settlement
- **Source:** Bloomberg via Reuters, Friday 20 November 2026.
- YouTube reached an in-principle agreement to potentially pay about
  $3 billion to thousands of US school districts that accused it of
  designing an addictive platform that harmed students' mental health —
  the largest-ever corporate settlement with US school districts. The
  deal includes product safeguards for teens.
